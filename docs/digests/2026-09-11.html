Language-Augmented Semantic Priors for B-Spline Surface Fitting

Yunzhong Lou, Yusheng Luo, Jiahao Li et al. · arXiv

What they studied: Researchers studied how to improve B-spline surface fitting in computer-aided design (CAD) systems.

Why they studied it: Traditional CAD systems often ignore design intent from modeling histories, leading to inconsistent surface fitting results.

Method: They developed LASP, a framework that uses large language models (LLMs) to infer B-spline prior parameters from textual descriptions of modeling histories.

Findings and results: Experiments showed that language-driven reasoning provides a strong inductive bias for geometric solving, leading to more accurate and semantically coherent B-spline fitting.

Benefits and impact: This approach directs the B-spline fitting process toward solutions that better reflect design objectives and improves semantic coherence in CAD.

Read the full breakdown

B-splines are fundamental to modern CAD systems. Current CAD tools often struggle to connect high-level design goals with the actual geometry they create. This can lead to surfaces that don't quite match the designer's original intent. The new framework, LASP, addresses this by using large language models. It translates a design's history into text, capturing details like design intent and how parts relate. An LLM then uses this information to predict better starting points for B-spline fitting. This helps existing CAD solvers produce geometry that is more aligned with the designer's vision.

Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws

Rongcan Pei, Zhepei Wei, Shuyao Xu et al. · arXiv

What they studied: Researchers studied how large language models (LLMs) improve through self-distillation.

Why they studied it: Current self-distillation methods can worsen LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks by suppressing uncertainty and self-correction.

Method: They developed Negative Self-Distillation (NSD), which teaches LLMs by having them avoid flawed reasoning instead of imitating correct answers.

Findings and results: NSD consistently outperformed On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) and other self-bootstrapping reinforcement learning methods.

Benefits and impact: This method improves LLM reasoning without needing ground-truth answers or external supervision.

Read the full breakdown

Large language models (LLMs) often use On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) to improve. OPSD teaches the model by having it imitate perfect reasoning steps. However, this can make LLMs worse at complex problems because it stops them from exploring and correcting themselves. Researchers introduced Negative Self-Distillation (NSD) to fix this. NSD teaches the model to avoid bad reasoning, rather than copy good reasoning. It uses a special mechanism to only target reasoning flaws, protecting the model's basic language skills. NSD performed better than OPSD and other similar methods.

.blend URL Viewer

Simon Willison · Simon Willison

What it covers: Simon Willison created a .blend URL Viewer tool. It displays Blender models in a web browser.

Why it matters now: This tool allows easy sharing and viewing of 3D models generated by AI. It demonstrates practical applications of AI in content creation.

Key takeaway: AI models like GPT-6 Astra can generate complex 3D models from images and text prompts. These models can be integrated with tools for viewing and sharing.

Benefits and impact: The tool simplifies the process of sharing Blender files. It makes AI-generated 3D content more accessible.

Read the full breakdown

Simon Willison developed a .blend URL Viewer. This tool allows users to view Blender 3D models directly in their web browser. Willison used ChatGPT Images 2.5 to generate an image of a Fabergé egg. He then fed this image to Codex running GPT-6 Astra. This AI model created several .blend files of the egg in 17 minutes and 51 seconds. The new viewer tool makes these AI-generated 3D models easily shareable.

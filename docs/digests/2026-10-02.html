Where-OPD: Spatially Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation of MLLMs with Synthetic Scenes

Sophia Sirko-Galouchenko, Monika Wysoczanska, Andrei Bursuc et al. · arXiv

What they studied: Researchers studied how to improve multimodal large language models (MLLMs) using a method called on-policy self-distillation.

Why they studied it: Applying self-distillation to MLLMs was largely unexplored, and existing methods for visual information had limitations.

Method: They developed a self-distillation method where a teacher MLLM receives textual, spatially grounded guidance about relevant visual elements in synthetic scenes.

Findings and results: This method consistently improved performance on counting, document, and chart understanding benchmarks, with a 3.23-point average gain on real-world perception benchmarks.

Benefits and impact: The approach enables scalable, annotation-free post-training and shows that improvements from synthetic data transfer to real-world tasks.

Read the full breakdown

This paper introduces a new way to train multimodal AI models, called Where-OPD. It uses a technique called on-policy self-distillation, where a model learns from a more informed version of itself. The teacher model gets extra information: text that describes where important objects are in an image. This training uses computer-generated images, so no human labeling is needed. Even though the training uses fake images, the improved models perform better on real-world tasks. This method boosts performance on tasks like counting objects and understanding documents and charts.

Mimir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control

Yimeng Liu, Mi Zhang, Younsuk Dong et al. · arXiv

What they studied: Researchers studied how large language model (LLM) agents perform in long-term physical control tasks, specifically irrigation.

Why they studied it: Most LLM agent research focuses on short tasks, but physical control involves compounding errors and learning from experience without changing physical rules.

Method: They developed Mimir, an LLM agent with two repair mechanisms: fast, numerical checking and revision of LLM outputs, and slow consolidation of failure patterns into persistent principles.

Findings and results: Mimir achieved the lowest control cost among references and used 51% less irrigation than historical schedules. Removing forward simulation, verified revision, or persistent context increased control costs.

Benefits and impact: This work shows how LLM agents can combine reasoning with self-improvement in physical systems while maintaining safety through numerical mechanisms.

Read the full breakdown

LLM agents are good at tasks with quick feedback, but controlling physical systems over long periods is different. Errors can build up, and agents must learn without changing the system's basic rules. Researchers created Mimir, an LLM agent designed for irrigation control. Mimir checks its decisions using a simulator and revises them before acting. It also learns from repeated mistakes, turning them into rules for future decisions. Mimir used 51% less water than historical methods and had lower control costs than other systems. This suggests LLM agents can manage complex physical systems effectively and safely.

Quoting Anthropic Frontier Red Team

Simon Willison · Simon Willison

What it covers: Anthropic's Frontier Red Team evaluated AI models on a cybersecurity benchmark. They tested models' ability to exploit software vulnerabilities.

Why it matters now: Newer models are showing capabilities in developing cyber exploits. This marks a significant increase in AI's ability to perform complex security tasks.

Key takeaway: Some advanced AI models can now create full control flow hijacks. Earlier models could not achieve this.

Benefits and impact: This research highlights the need for advanced AI safety measures. It shows AI models are gaining sophisticated offensive cyber capabilities.

Read the full breakdown

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team tested AI models using 100 tasks from an internal Binary Exploitation benchmark. They found that GLM-5.3 developed full control flow hijacks in 4% of trials. Claude Mythos Preview achieved this in 6% of trials. Older models, such as Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2, did not succeed in any trials. This indicates a new threshold for AI capabilities in cybersecurity exploitation has been crossed.

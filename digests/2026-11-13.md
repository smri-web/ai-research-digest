### LLMON: An LLM-Native Markup Language, now in Granite 4.1 3B and 8B
*Hugging Face · Hugging Face* · [https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite)

- **What it covers:** IBM has released LLMON, a new markup language for Large Language Models (LLMs). It is now available in Granite 4.1 3B and 8B models.
- **Why it matters now:** LLMON helps LLMs understand and process structured information more effectively. This improves their ability to follow instructions and generate accurate outputs.
- **Key takeaway:** LLMON provides a standardized way for LLMs to handle complex data structures. This makes LLMs more reliable for specific tasks.
- **Benefits and impact:** This can lead to better performance in tasks requiring precise data extraction and generation. It also simplifies the interaction between users and LLMs for structured outputs.

## AI system design & engineering

### AgentSSL: Can MLE Agents Leverage Unlabeled Data?
*Ethan Lin, Akanksha Sarkar, Ziang Liu et al. · arXiv* · [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17323v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17323v1)

- **What they studied:** Researchers studied if machine learning agents can use unlabeled data to improve performance.
- **Why they studied it:** Current machine learning agents need a lot of labeled data, which is hard to get in new areas.
- **Method:** They framed semi-supervised learning as a program search problem for agents.
- **Findings and results:** Agents outperformed human-engineered methods in 8 of 12 settings, improving by up to 21.8%. Program selection was a major bottleneck, reducing performance by up to 47%.
- **Benefits and impact:** This work shows agents can adapt existing techniques and perform well with limited labeled data.

### Scalable Conversational Assessment: Chat-Based Exams with a Two-Agent LLM Pipeline
*Geoffrey Challen, Craig Zilles · arXiv* · [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17220v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17220v1)

- **What they studied:** Researchers studied how to create scalable, interactive exams using AI.
- **Why they studied it:** Oral exams are effective for assessing student reasoning but do not scale well.
- **Method:** They developed a two-agent LLM system for text-based conversational assessments. One agent interviews the student, and a hidden evaluator guides the interview and tracks student progress.
- **Findings and results:** The system was tested with simulated students and deployed in a small AI literacy course. Review of transcripts identified areas for improving follow-up questions and ensuring accurate grading.
- **Benefits and impact:** This system offers a way to conduct interactive assessments at scale, addressing a limitation of traditional oral exams.

*Jeremy Hsu · Ars Technica* · [https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/)

- **What it covers:** The FAA is expanding its use of an AI tool to manage air traffic congestion across the entire US airspace. This tool aims to improve efficiency in air traffic management.
- **Why it matters now:** Airlines have praised the AI tool, indicating potential benefits for the industry. However, the air traffic controllers' union was not involved in its development or deployment.
- **Key takeaway:** The FAA is broadening AI application for national air traffic control, with airline support but without union input.
- **Benefits and impact:** The AI tool is expected to help manage air traffic congestion, potentially leading to smoother operations for airlines and travelers.
